Mailroom v0.7 · design exploration

Mail a shared memory.
Make that the whole app.

v0.6.x lets users browse the app for a while before sending anything. They can wander into the Constellation, the Map, the friends list, the profile, the about-me editor — and never get to the point. The product's job is one thing: mail a real postcard of a shared memory. Everything else is scaffolding. v0.7 makes that the gravitational center of the app.

Below: design principles, three concrete layout directions with mockups, a clear what-to-cut table, four magical moments worth building, and a recommendation.

First principles

Notion's discipline. Snapchat's obviousness. Postal warmth.

1

The send button is the camera

Snapchat opens straight to the shutter. Mailroom should open straight to "mail something." Send isn't a destination, it's the home state.

2

The profile is your archive

Not a CV with interests/birthday/about-me. A journal of the memories you've sent and received, stacked week by week. Empty weeks read as "send something."

3

Cut everything that doesn't serve the first send

Constellation, map, address book are great surfaces for returning users. They are friction for first-time users. Hide them until earned.

4

Say the why out loud, once

"Mail a shared memory." That's the tagline, the empty-state copy, the send-confirmation copy, the share copy. Same five words, everywhere.

Direction 1

Send Bar The gentle push.

4 tabs (Postcards · People · Map · Profile). The Postcards tab is your home. Persistent black "Mail a memory →" bar pinned at the top of every Postcards view. Below it: your week-by-week journal. About-me is moved entirely into Settings as a single bio textarea. Constellation lives inside the People tab as a view toggle (List · Constellation), so it's never more than one tap deeper than today.

Best for: users who like the browseable feel of v0.6.x but need a clear, persistent reminder of what the app is FOR.

Direction 2 · recommended

Floating Send The Escargot move.

4 tabs (Postcards · People · Map · You) plus an oversized floating send button dead-center of the tab bar, slightly raised. The button is the loudest thing on screen at all times. The Postcards tab is the journal of everything you've sent and received. Constellation lives inside the People tab as a view toggle. same beautiful visualization, just one tap deeper instead of consuming a whole tab slot. Profile is just bio + streak + settings.

Why this wins: matches the pattern your Escargot screenshot uses and that Snap, Instagram, BeReal all share: the create action is a button, not a tab. Users never lose the path to sending.

Direction 3

Three Tabs Hyper-condense.

Three tabs: Send · Postcards · People. Send is the default tab on launch — the app opens to the photo picker. Mail is the journal. People merges Friends + Map + Constellation behind a segmented toggle. Profile is the avatar in the top-right of every screen. About-me is gone; bio lives in Settings.

Best for: "obviousness and ease" maxed out. The user can't open the app without seeing the send action first. But it demotes the journal and map from tabs, which is a bigger leap from v0.6.x.

What changes

What to cut, what to move, what to keep.

The audit below is identical across all three directions — just the delivery differs. v0.7 ships less, not more.

Surface Verdict Why
About me grid (interests / send-me / birthday / currently-into) cut Four optional fields that gate nothing and feel like a profile-completion chore. Replace with one bio textarea behind Settings.
Bio — single textarea new One free-form sentence about you. Lives in Settings > Edit Bio. Shows on your profile + on the back of cards you send (optional).
Profile as primary surface move Profile is now a thin sheet: avatar, bio, three stats, link to Settings. The mail journal moves to its own Postcards tab.
Postcards journal (week-by-week gallery) new (revived) Owns the Postcards tab (renamed from "Mail" so it doesn't collide with the verb "mail it" used in the send flow). Each row is a week, horizontally scrollable. Inbound has a blue ring, outbound a red stamp. Empty trailing tile is a CTA back to Send.
Constellation keep (moved) Not deleted. The visualization stays exactly as it is — it moves inside the People tab as one of the segmented view modes (List · Constellation). Same visual; same data; same magic. Just one tap deeper instead of consuming a top-level tab slot. Bonus: more users discover it because the toggle is right above the friend list.
Map keep Strongest moat surface — a real visual reward for sending. Stays as a top-level tab in Directions 1 + 2; folds into People in Direction 3.
Pen pal / send-to-stranger new You called it out — never wired up. First-class recipient option in the Send modal AND as a row in the People list. Match by mood, vibe, or city. Opt-in to receive.
"First Card Ideas" / category grid cut Already cut in v0.5.0. Stays cut. A photo + a note is the whole product.
"Mailroom mails real postcards" pause page cut Already cut in v0.6.1 build 8. Hero tagline does the same work in two seconds.
Send Mail / Add Friend buttons on profile cut Already cut in v0.6.1 build 8. Redundant with tab bar / FAB.
"Friends" tab name → "People" rename The tab's job widens slightly: friend list plus Constellation view plus the new Pen Pal entry point. "People" covers all three; "Friends" is too narrow.
Streak indicator ("7 weeks in a row") new The single metric most likely to drive habitual sending. Shown in profile + at the top of the Postcards journal. Soft framing, not a chore.
Magical moments

Four small things that make this feel alive.

None of these are screens. They're micro-moments inside screens that earn the brand and reward sending. Cheap to build, disproportionately sticky.

✉

The fold

On tap-Mail-it: the photo + note compose into a postcard, then a paper-fold animation folds the card into an envelope. ~700ms. The same moment you've been waiting all your life to see in an app.

📍

The pin drops

When Lob confirms a card is in transit, a postal-red dot animates onto the Map at the destination city. When delivered, the dot solidifies. A small thing. Users will keep checking.

🌟

The reciprocation badge

When someone you mailed first mails you back, their card lands in your journal with a soft gold ring + "Your pen pal" stamp. The loop closing is the dopamine.

💬

The empty-week whisper

If you go a week without sending, the trailing tile in this week's row morphs from a quiet dashed plus into a soft black tile that says “Mail someone →”. No notification. Just a nudge inside the journal you'll already check.

Recommendation

Ship Direction 2: Floating Send.

Direction 2 lands on the right tradeoff. It keeps the surfaces you said “have a time and place” (Mail, Friends, Map, You) as tabs — so users who want to wander still can — while making the send action the loudest, biggest, most-pressed thing on every screen. It's the Escargot pattern, the Snapchat pattern, the Instagram pattern. It's a known win.

Direction 3 is more aggressive and more in line with your Snapchat analogy, but demoting the journal and the map from tabs is a big structural reshuffle that affects the constellation, the city-map moat, and existing user muscle memory. It's a v0.8 evolution, not a v0.7 release.

Direction 1 is the safest. The send bar at the top is honest and visible, but a bar is always weaker than a button.

Concrete v0.7 scope, in order

  1. Build the floating Send FAB + camera-style modal (replaces the current Send screen + its 4-step gallery).
  2. Move the existing mail history to be the Postcards tab's journal (week-by-week, sent + received unified).
  3. Delete About-me grid. Replace with a single Bio textarea in Settings > Edit Bio.
  4. Wire stranger / pen-pal send: a recipient row in the send modal + a People-list row that opens a "match a pen pal" sheet.
  5. Add streak count to profile + Mail header.
  6. Build the four magical moments: fold, pin drop, reciprocation badge, empty-week whisper.
  7. Constellation kept — moves inside the renamed People tab as a segmented view toggle (List · Constellation). Map stays as its own top-level tab.
  • Single tagline everywhere: “Mail a shared memory.”
  • One bio textarea, no profile grid
  • Floating Send is the camera
  • Empty weeks recruit
