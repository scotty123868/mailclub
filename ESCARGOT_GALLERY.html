Mailroom · Product Direction · May 11, 2026

Run at this our own way.

Escargot is louder, faster, more viral. Mailroom is quieter, warmer, and built on a different idea: that sending real mail is its own reward. We were starting to mirror their playbook. We're not going to.

The pivot

What we're not doing.

A clean list of proposals from the prior gallery that are off the table after the conversation. Each was rejected for the same underlying reason: pressuring the user is not our voice. Mailroom should feel like an empty postcard waiting for you, not a notification trying to extract a behavior.

Killed · virality pressure

Every friend = a free card

Distracts from the magic of "you have 3 free postcards to send right now." The user came to send mail, not to be a growth vector.

Killed · speed pressure

"Mail a card in 60 seconds"

Quantifying speed reframes the act as a chore to dispatch. Mailroom isn't fast food. The whole point is that someone took the time.

Killed · time pressure

First-card welcome offer with 24h countdown

Manufactured urgency for a product whose currency is sincerity. A countdown on a postcard app is uncanny.

Killed · task pressure

Friend birthdays as a Todo list

Birthdays should be a soft signal, not an inbox. We collect them so cards can land on the right day, not so the user feels nagged into sending them.

Killed · gating

Map locked until first send

Replaced. The empty map should explain itself, not gate itself. New copy: "Send your first card. Its path will show up here." Visible map, no overlay, no quest.

Killed · vibe theft

Escargot styling, tone, framing

Their playbook is right for them. Their copy, color, and humor are not ours. We don't need to argue with them — we're not in the same conversation.

The philosophy

Utility first. Memory archive second. No third thing.

The whole product is two promises. The first promise gets them in. The second promise keeps them.

Promise 1
Free sends, no friction. You get a small starter balance on signup — enough to use today, right now, with one photo from your library and one note. The app exists to make that easy. Open: 3 or 5 starters? Current code is 5; your recent framing said "3 free postcards." Pick before next build.
Promise 2
Lasting memory. The photo you cared enough to mail is a photo you cared enough to keep. Mailroom remembers them for you — a quiet archive of moments you sent into someone else's life.
No automatic credit grants
The starter balance is the only time credits appear without the user buying them. No welcome-offer bonus, no referral-on-tap, no streak rewards, no "complete your profile" credits. The balance is just what you have, like a fresh notebook — not a slot machine.
What we don't do
Pressure. Streaks. Daily quests. Notification badges. Referral nags. Achievements. Counter-resets. Bonus-credit moments. None of it. The mail itself is the reward.

Your draft was "make memories momentos." Keeping the structure exactly — just one letter to fix. Momentos is Spanish/Portuguese for "moments." The English word for a small keepsake — the thing a printed postcard is — is mementos. Same M-M rhythm, real meaning.

M-M-M three-beat. Reads as a directive: turn memories into things you can hold. Crisp, unique, ours.

The big add

The Mail Card — a retroactive archive.

Borrowed in spirit from your teteapp's MomentCardHero pattern — the map-prominent journal card. Adapted for Mailroom: every send becomes a Mail Card that survives in a gallery. By week, scrollable, tappable, mappable. Two surfaces: a list view (the journal) and a map view (the routes). Tapping a route shows a peek card at the bottom — like teteapp's `MomentPeek`.

How it composes (no new tab needed)

The new structure threads into what's already on disk. No extra navigation. Constellation becomes Mail — same tab slot, new purpose. The map tab keeps its slot but gets the peek-card mechanic. Detail view is a new screen that reuses PostcardFrontPreview + PostcardBackPreview at a larger size.

What happens to the Friends tab

Friends gets richer. Three pieces land here:

  1. Constellation insights — "warmest," "newest," "sleeping 60+ days" — move from the retired Constellation tab to small badges on the friend rows, or a header strip above the list.
  2. Upcoming birthdays section — a quiet list of friends whose birthdays fall in the next 60 days, sorted nearest-first. Not a task list. No urgency CTAs in the row. Just a list. Tap a friend → existing friend detail. The user decides whether to send. The birthdays surface because they're useful, not because we're prompting.
  3. Existing friend list — unchanged. The "All / Close / New" filter chips stay; we can optionally add a "Birthday soon" chip.

Friends becomes the single home for everything about people. Birthdays live here because they're a property of friends — not a separate task surface, not a home-screen interruption.

Data model — what's already there

The postcards array in MailClubContext already has { sentAt, toFriendId, toCity, fromCity, photoUri, message, status } per send. The gallery and detail views are pure derivations from this — no schema change. The map already derives routes from this same array. Adding the peek-card mechanic is UI-only on top of `MapPanel`. About 3 days of engineering total.

Small fixes from the original gallery that survive

The few things still worth doing.

Most of the original 10 proposals got cut. A handful are still right — but only because they support the utility-first philosophy, not because they came from a competitor.

Soft map empty state
Replace the current blank map with one line of cream-paper italic: "Send your first card. Its path will show up here." No overlay, no lock, no quest. Just a sentence that tells the user what the surface will do once they use it.
Delivery transparency on send
Between the recipient picker and the Send button, a quiet line: "We'll print Tuesday · arrives ~May 18." Real dates from Lob's SLA. This is information, not pressure. Sets the expectation so the wait doesn't feel broken.
Stamp-frame option on the photo
Toggle, off by default. A small set of postal frames (1¢ red stamp scallop, polaroid white border, washi tape). For people who want it. For people who don't, the raw photo stays. Pulls forward the Mailroom DNA without forcing it.
"Mail a memory" as the App Store hero
Replaces the abstract brand poetry on the first App Store screenshot. Doesn't quantify speed. Doesn't promise virality. Just names the thing the product does.
Mailroom's existing strengths · do not regress

What Mailroom already has that escargot doesn't.

Keep · brand

Coherent physical-mail identity

Stamps, postmarks, cream paper, serif, the 1¢ red stamp — every screen reads as one product. Escargot's identity shifts screen-to-screen.

Keep · privacy

Send-a-link to an unknown address

The "Ask" recipient mode is a real moat. Recipient fills in their address privately, sender never sees it. Nobody else does this.

Keep · honest data

Routes derived from real sends

Our map shows what actually happened. No fake pins, no synthetic activity. The new peek card makes this stronger.

Keep · prompts

Card-idea prompts on home

"Send me the photo from tonight," "Send me your favorite place." These are interaction starters, not pressure. They survive the new direction unchanged.

Still open

Decide before next build.

  1. Starter balance: 3 or 5? Current code grants 5. Your earlier framing said "3 free postcards." 3 is tighter — feels more like a gift, drives the second send to be a paid pack. 5 is what the WelcomeSheet copy already promises ("5 free postcards on us"). Pick before next build so the copy and the math agree.
  2. Photo-less sends, ever? Today every send has a photo. If we someday allow text-only postcards, the Mail Card thumbnail needs a fallback — handwritten-script render of the first line of the note, or a solid stamp tile. Defer until we know we want them.
  3. Friend birthday source. Where does a friend's birthday come from? (a) your own iOS contacts permission, (b) the friend filling it in via the "Ask" magic-link, (c) the friend's own Mailroom profile. (a) is densest but most invasive; (b) and (c) are cleaner. My lean: start with (c) only — the friend opted into Mailroom, they can put their own birthday in their profile. Add (a) as an explicit opt-in toggle later if friend-graph density becomes the bottleneck.
  4. Privacy of recipient's street address. The Mail Card detail shows "Brooklyn, NY" but never the line1 / line2 / ZIP. Worth a one-line audit pass to confirm none of those fields ever land in the gallery view, even for the sender who originally typed them.
