What changes vs today

Today (v0.5.0 build 5): WelcomeSheet is one dense screen with name, city, state, birthday, Apple Sign In, and an email fallback all stacked. Functional but cramped, and the birthday ask up-front is friction nobody asked for.

Tomorrow (v0.6.0): one decision per page. Option B hero art ("Cards across the map") on page 1 carries the brand promise visually with the new CTA "Send a postcard with a text." Name on page 2, an explanation moment on page 3, address autocomplete on page 4 (single bar that suggests as you type), 3-stamps confirmation on page 5. No birthday.

Pen-pal feature is Postcrossing applied to Mailroom: send a card to a stranger, get one back. About 2 weeks. Both ways are blind. you don't know where your card is going, the recipient doesn't know who sent theirs. Reply by scanning the QR if you want to upgrade the connection.

No virtual mail. The receiver's app NEVER shows them mail before it physically arrives at their address. No pending counter, no in-app pre-notification. The card surprises them in their real mailbox, they scan the QR on the back, and only then does it land in their app feed. Preserves the entire point.

04Pen-pal · Mail a stranger. Get one back.

Postcrossing's mechanic, applied to Mailroom. You mail one. Someone in the network mails you one. Not necessarily the same person. the matching engine handles it. Existing sendIntoVoid + void_replies infrastructure forms the foundation. No separate "Pen-pal mailbox". when a card arrives at your physical mailbox and you scan the QR, it appears in your normal Received feed like any other card.

How the get-one-back works under the hood

Every pen-pal send earns the sender a pending receive credit on the server. The pen-pal pool is a queue of senders who haven't yet received a card. When someone else picks "Mail a stranger," their card routes to the next eligible sender. ~2 week SLA from send to arrival.

Backfill: if a card doesn't reach its destination (return-to-sender, lost mail) within ~30 days, the next stranger send routes to that pending receiver. If the queue ever runs dry, Mailroom-managed staff cards fill the gap.

Implementation: new penpal_queue table (sender_id, sent_at, fulfilled_at, fulfilled_by_postcard_id). Cron-style edge function runs daily.

No virtual cards. preserve the surprise

The receiver's app NEVER tells them mail is on the way. No "pending" counter, no in-app pre-notification. The card appears in the Received feed only after it's PHYSICALLY DELIVERED and the recipient has scanned the QR on the back. Lob's delivery webhook flips a delivered_at timestamp on the postcard row; the receiver's fetch_received_postcards query filters to only show rows where delivered_at IS NOT NULL (or where the user has scanned, whichever comes first).

Senders can see their own sent card's progress on their existing Sent list (queued → in-transit → delivered) since that's their own postcard, not a surprise to ruin.
