Mailroom postcard back. four options

Each mock-up is rendered at print scale (600×408px = 6.25"×4.25" at 96dpi). The pale red dashed rectangles mark Lob's protected zones. the address block + IMb barcode area where Lob auto-prints. We can't put any ink in those zones; they're shown here only so you understand the design constraint.

What to evaluate: Does the stamp art feel like Mailroom? Where does your eye go first? Does the QR feel like part of the brand or like a tacked-on QR? Read the message. does the layout get out of the way? If you got this in the mail and didn't know it was an app, what would you think the sender was telling you?

Option A. Mirror Retro

QR LEFT · STAMP RIGHT · SEAL CORNER

Direct copy of Retro's anatomy with our envelope-balloon art. QR + sender handle top-left, big balloon stamp top-right, round "for the fridge not the feed" seal further right. Familiar but ours.

Option B. Bold M wordmark

BRAND-FORWARD · TYPE ANCHOR · 70¢ SERIAL

The "M" tile (marketing-site logo) is the brand anchor, not the stamp. Big wordmark dominates top-left, QR drops below as metadata. Stamp top-right with serial price "PRSRT · 70¢". postage-stamp authenticity.

Option C. Vintage purist

PERFORATED STAMP · WAX SEAL · CENTER DIVIDER

Stamp-centric. Top-right stamp has real perforated edges, Playfair Display, "70¢" denomination. feels like an actual postage stamp. QR becomes a small wax seal "M". Classic USPS center divider preserves postcard tradition.

Option D. Minimal modern

TRUST THE MESSAGE · NO CHROME · 70% SPACE FOR INK

Less is more. Sender's name as a single type line (no big tile). Small stamp top-right. QR shrinks to 36×36 bottom-left, almost an afterthought. The handwritten message gets the canvas. Reads like a personal note, not a marketing surface.
